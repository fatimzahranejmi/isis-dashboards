USCIS can now require e-filing on immigration benefit requests, starting today. If you represent clients, that functions as a mandate on how your firm files.

DHS published an interim final rule effective August 11, 2026 that gives USCIS discretion to require electronic filing for any form that's already been e-fileable for 180 days or more. USCIS still has to post 60 days' public notice before mandating e-filing on any specific form. But the legal authority is in place now, and it's worth getting ahead of.

A few things worth knowing:
→ 22 forms are already e-fileable today, including I-130, I-131, I-539, I-765, N-400, and I-90. Those forms have already cleared the 180-day threshold, so USCIS could mandate e-filing on them at any time.

→ Don't count on a waiver as your backup plan. A new form, I-936, lets a requestor ask for a waiver from e-filing due to undue hardship ($25 fee). But DHS has said plainly it doesn't expect represented clients or business filers to generally qualify. Its reasoning: once someone retains counsel, internet access is assumed to run through the firm, not the client personally. That puts the compliance burden on your firm's systems, not your client's.

→ A waiver request doesn't hold anyone's place in line. If the receipt date matters, whether for a deadline or an oversubscribed category, that date is set when USCIS actually receives the paper filing
